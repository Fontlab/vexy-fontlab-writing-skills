# Balanced writing: worked cases

## Worked example: a supported possibility

Brief: write a short balanced introduction to masks in Vexy Lines for an
illustrator. The supplied facts are: masks are vector stencils; transparent
areas reveal fills and opaque areas hide them; a portrait can use separate
fills on the face, hair, and background. No speed claim or steps are supplied.

Draft:

> Give the face, hair, and background their own drawing styles. In Vexy Lines,
> you can use a mask to keep each layer's fill within its own region.
>
> A mask is a vector stencil. Transparent areas reveal the fill; opaque areas
> hide it. For a portrait, use separate masked layers for the face, hair, and
> background, with a different fill on each.

The opening invites a concrete creative choice. “Each layer's fill” connects
that choice to the mechanism. The next paragraph defines the same object and
shows its use. It adds no claim about speed, automatic recognition, or ease.
This is a concept introduction, not a verified click-by-click procedure.

## Worked example: preserve an inconvenient boundary

This is a hypothetical brief for a fictional product called Proof Viewer:
compare two supplied previews side by side; export is unavailable; no price,
download route, or time saving is documented.

Draft:

> Compare the two previews side by side. Proof Viewer displays both previews
> together, so you can inspect their differences.
>
> Proof Viewer does not export files.

The action carries the appeal, the mechanism explains it, and the limitation
stays explicit. Do not replace the last line with a positive claim about
“focused reviewing”. No emotional bridge is needed before a clear limitation.
