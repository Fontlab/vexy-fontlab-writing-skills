# Neutral voice: worked pairs

Every "after" is either a real sentence from the house corpus or a rewrite that invents nothing the "before" did not contain.

## 1. The opening

**Before**
> In today's fast-moving type design landscape, FontLab 8 represents a significant step forward in variable font technology, offering designers an unprecedented level of control.

**After**
> FontLab is uncompromisingly variable-first.

Five words. A positioning claim with no hedge, subject FontLab, present tense. Thirteen of thirteen house essays open like this: one short declarative, never a scene, never a problem, never a question.

## 2. Paragraph two

**Before**
> FontLab offers a comprehensive suite of tools designed to streamline the glyph construction process, enabling users to work more efficiently through a variety of automation features.

**After**
> Rapidly build glyphs from components or from always-editable element references. Automate complex glyphs with Auto layers. Join design parts and add flair with Skin and Glue. Convert drawing parts into components with one click.

Four imperative sentences, four real tool names, no connective tissue. The house overview paragraph is a capability block, not a topic sentence with support.

## 3. A changed feature

**Before**
> The harmonized dragging feature has been improved and is now available across all platforms, bringing parity to the user experience.

**After**
> Previously, harmonized dragging was only available on macOS. It now works on Windows too.

`Previously, ... now ...` appears 14 times in the corpus and is the house's only comparison device. Before and After, The old way and The new way, and Today versus With FontLab appear zero times in 76,000 words.

## 4. A new behaviour, with the benefit welded on

**Before**
> The Commands & Shortcuts dialog has been redesigned. This means you can find commands faster and spend less time hunting through menus.

**After**
> When you choose Tools > Commands & Shortcuts, the dialog now opens as a compact popup, so you can type a substring of a menu command and choose it from the list.

Condition, then behaviour, then the benefit welded with "so you can". The house never gives a benefit its own sentence, and "This means you can spend less time on X" is the shape to delete on sight.

## 5. The rationale

**Before**
> The colors of the panels and interface areas have been made more uniform in order to create a more cohesive and less distracting visual environment for extended work sessions.

**After**
> The colors of the panels and UI areas are more uniform, to reduce visual noise.

Four words of rationale. That is the house length.

## 6. A fix

**Before**
> Fixed a null pointer dereference in the contour simplification code path.

**After**
> Fixed a crash when simplifying a contour with a single off-curve point. The cause was a null dereference in the simplification routine.

Symptom first, because that is what the reader recognises. The cause can follow for the reader who wants it.

## 7. A limitation

**Before**
> TransType focuses on delivering rock-solid static font output, with variable font support on the roadmap.

**After**
> TransType does not export a new variable font.

Converting a limitation into a positioning is the most damaging edit available in this register, because it is the one a reader can catch you at.

## 8. The ending

**Before**
> With these improvements, FontLab 8 continues to set the standard for professional font editing, giving designers the tools they need to bring their vision to life.

**After**
> Numerous additional fixes for problems reported by users.

Zero of thirteen house essays end with a summary or a call to action. They end on the last fact, however flat. A terminal paragraph that adds no new fact is the single most reliable machine tell here.

## 9. The long sentence that must survive

**Leave alone**
> Dynamically snap to zones, guides, hints, nodes, angles, stem distances, continuation lines, perpendicular lines and centerlines.

Twenty-two words, nine items, no commas spared. Fifteen percent of house sentences run over 30 words. A readability pass that caps sentences at 25 words destroys the register.

## 10. The single-sentence paragraph

**Leave alone**
> Previously, this operation was called Harmonize Handles in the context menu, but now Harmonize Handles does G2-smoothing of an existing node by moving its handles.

One sentence carrying an entire rename history, standing alone as a paragraph. Between 45 and 57 percent of house paragraphs are a single sentence. Merging them into developed paragraphs is a rewrite, not an edit.

## 11. The aside an editor removes

**Leave alone**
> Or you may just assign Alt+Enter to Node: Toggle Smooth, it's up to you!

A comma splice, an exclamation mark, and a shrug. All three are doing work. Fix a typo if asked; do not repair the rhythm.

## 12. The exclamation that belongs

**Leave alone**
> True multi-color OpenType fonts!

The house runs about one exclamation per 400 words in overview and announcement prose, and about one per 4,000 in reference prose. A blanket ban is wrong by a factor of ten in the first case.
