# Marketing voice: worked pairs

## 1. The headline

**Before**
> Unlock Your Creative Potential with the Industry's Most Powerful Font Editor

**After**
> Design & edit OpenType, variable, web & color fonts

Imperative plus object, no adjective, bare ampersands. The house has four headline shapes and this is the commonest.

## 2. The feature list

**Before**
> FontLab provides a comprehensive set of refinement tools that empower designers to perfect their letterforms with unprecedented precision and efficiency.

**After**
> **Refine** your drawings: create **overlaps**, **simplify** paths, **equalize** stems. **Scale** while **keeping** stroke **thickness**, globally **adjust** weight and width, **find & fix** imperfections.

Nine bolded verbs, plain nouns, one colon, zero adjectives of praise. The benefit is the verb. This is the single most characteristic move in the house marketing voice, and a consistency pass that re-bolds the product nouns will kill it.

## 3. The whole product in one breath

**Before**
> TransType streamlines your font conversion workflow with an intuitive interface that makes converting between formats effortless, no matter your level of experience.

**After**
> Drop fonts in. Pick a format. Click Convert. Done.

Twelve words. Four beats, not three: the house drumroll is a heading device and it is not always a tricolon.

## 4. Proof

**Before**
> Trusted by leading type designers and foundries worldwide, FontLab has become the industry standard for professional font production.

**After**
> Thousands of designers and foundries large and small have been using the FontLab apps to create 10,000s professional fonts: Adobe, Apple, FontFont, Linotype, Microsoft, Monotype, Canada Type, Porchez, Underware, Tiro Typeworks and many more.

A colon, then eleven named companies. Proof by enumeration, never by adjective. "Trusted by" without names is the sentence a competitor could paste unchanged.

## 5. The audacious claim

**Leave alone**
> Most fonts that are bundled with Microsoft Windows or with the Apple systems were designed in our apps!

One sentence, one exclamation, no supporting paragraph. The house states its biggest claim and moves on. An editor will want to build a paragraph around it, which is how a strong claim starts to read like a weak one.

## 6. The testimonial

**Before**
> Our users consistently praise FontLab's vector engine, describing it as best-in-class and noting that it continues to impress even seasoned professionals.

**After**
> "Best vector engine," they say. "Continues to impress," they say.

Quoted verbatim, attributed, unframed, and the repetition kept. The house never paraphrases a customer and never writes an introduction to its own testimonials.

## 7. The simile

**Leave alone**
> Space and kern in multi-line tabs or windows that feel like a text editor.

The only simile on the page, and it compares to a familiar tool rather than to an emotion. That is the permitted shape.

## 8. The dash

**Before**
> FontLab 8 is a complete font editor — powerful, precise, professional.

**After**
> FontLab 8 is a complete font editor. It is precise, and it is fast, and it has been shipping since before webfonts existed.

The first is the appositive gloss dash: a noun phrase, a dash, and two or three adjectives of atmosphere. It is the machine tell, and it accounts for most of the dashes on the pages an AI wrote for this company. The permitted dash turns the sentence instead:

> It's easier to learn and use, it's better, it's faster — and most importantly, it's way, way more stable and reliable.

What follows the dash carries a finite verb and changes the direction of the sentence.

## 9. The question

**Before**
> Ever wondered what your workflow could look like without the constant format juggling?

**After**
> Ready for the next level?

A question addressed to a state of readiness, standing as a heading, answered by the section under it. Rhetorical questions in body copy appear zero times in the house corpus.

## 10. What not to explain

**Before**
> Kerning is the process of adjusting the space between two adjacent letters so that the text appears evenly spaced.

**After**
> Kerning works on classes, so one exception fixes every pair that shares the same shapes.

The reader is shopping for a font editor. Explaining kerning to them costs you the sentence you needed for what your kerning does differently.

## Sentences to delete on sight

"In today's fast-paced design world." "Unlock the potential of your type." "Take your workflow to the next level." "We are excited to announce." "Trusted by industry leaders." "It's not just a font editor, it's a platform." Each survives the deletion of the sentence around it, which is the proof it was carrying nothing.
