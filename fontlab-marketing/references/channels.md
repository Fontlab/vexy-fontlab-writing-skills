# Channels: email, video, ads, comparisons, search and answer engines

Fictional packets unless marked verified.

## Message match across a route

[Hypothetical packet] Facts: an ad offers “a 30-day trial of the master view”.
The landing page has a trial button. Checkout shows the trial terms.

> **Ad:** See every master of a glyph side by side. 30-day trial.
>
> **Landing headline:** Every master of a glyph, side by side: try it for 30 days
>
> **Checkout line:** 30-day trial, all features, watermark on export.

The promise does not change shape between click and checkout. A landing page
headed “The future of type design” would make the reader search for the thing
they clicked.

## A welcome email that keeps its promise

Facts: the reader downloaded a fictional sample-file pack. The list sends one
email a month. Replies reach a named support person.

> **Subject:** Your sample files, and what to try first
>
> Here are the five sample files you asked for: [CONFIRM DESTINATION: download link].
> Open `01-regular.ufo` first; it has the master view set up.
>
> You will get one email a month: one technique, release notes when there is
> a release. Reply to this message if a file does not open; it reaches Anna in
> support. [CONFIRM: sender and reply route]

Deliver the promised thing in the first lines, say what comes next and how
often, and make replies real.

## Mostly useful, offers labelled

A monthly newsletter can teach, show a technique, round up outside work and,
when there is one, make an offer. Label the offer as an offer:

> **Subject:** Offer: version 5 upgrade at EUR 49 until 30 November

## A first frame that is the work

Facts: a short clip shows one photograph turned into three line styles in a
fictional line-art app.

> **First frame:** the three results side by side, with the caption “One photo,
> three line styles”.
>
> **Next shot:** the source photo, then the style menu.

Avoid opening on a logo, a greeting or a face announcing that something amazing
is coming. The work is the hook; the next shot pays it.

## An ad that teaches

> Making a variable font? Incompatible masters are easy to miss by eye. Open
> the master view to see them side by side. 30-day trial.

One angle, one object, one action. It answers “will this help with my job?”
inside the ad.

## A fair comparison page

Facts: a fictional editor compared with another named editor on stated
criteria. We make the first.

> **We make Editor A. This page compares it with Editor B by task.**
>
> | Task | Editor A | Editor B |
> |---|---|---|
> | Variable font export | Yes | Yes |
> | Scripting language | Python | Python |
> | Platforms | macOS, Windows | macOS |
> | License | Perpetual, paid upgrades | [CONFIRM: from Editor B's site, dated] |
>
> **Choose Editor B if** you work only on macOS and already use its plug-ins.
> **Choose Editor A if** you need Windows or want to keep a perpetual license.

Disclose who wrote the page, state the criteria, describe the other product by
its own published facts, date the table and say when the other tool fits
better. No adjectives about the competitor.

## Passages that survive being quoted

> **Question heading:** Does Vexy Lines export SVG?
>
> **Answer first:** Yes. Vexy Lines exports SVG, PDF and EPS as vector formats,
> and PNG and JPEG as raster images.

(The format list follows the Vexy Lines manual pages; the current landing page
lists fewer formats. Recheck both before publication.)

The answer names the product, so it stays true when an answer engine lifts it
out of the page. “Yes, it does” would not.

## One product sentence, everywhere

Draft a single approved description and reuse it verbatim on the product page,
store listing, video description and press kit:

> Vexy Lines turns a bitmap image into line artwork you can edit and export as
> vector files. [CONFIRM: approved product sentence]

Varying the description for freshness teaches readers and answer engines that
there are several products.

## Publish the facts yourself

> **Is there a free version?** The demo is free to download. Exporting your
> artwork requires a license. [CONFIRM OFFER: current demo and license terms]

If our own page does not state the price and the demo terms, readers and
answer engines will take them from an old forum thread.

## Answering in a community

Fictional exchange about a fictional editor:

> I work on Editor A. The kerning disappears because the feature file is not
> being merged on export [CONFIRM: cause]. In the export profile, turn on
> [CONFIRM LABEL: option].
> If that does not fix it, send the file to support and mention this thread.

Disclose the affiliation in the first line, put the whole answer in the reply
and link only when the linked page adds something.
