# Claims, disclosure and tests on buyers

Load this file when a brief involves a comparison, a quotation, a "free" or
discounted offer, a partner or creator post, a sponsored segment, or a request
to test copy. The legal notes summarise what writers need to notice; they are
not legal advice, and the questions they raise go to the owner of the offer.

## A claim needs its evidence before the sentence

Keep one row per factual claim the copy uses: the claim, every reasonable
reading of it, the evidence, the version tested, the date and who tested it.
Write only sentences that have a row; otherwise describe the mechanism or mark
`[VERIFY CLAIM: evidence needed]`.

| Draft | Reading a buyer can take | What the copy needs |
|---|---|---|
| "Opens every format a type designer meets" | a complete list | the enumerated format list |
| "Saves hours on every family" | a measured time saving | a timed test on a real task, or the mechanism: "spacing and kerning suggestions that run on your computer" |
| "The only converter that ..." | a survey of the market | evidence of the market, or drop "only" |
| "Your settings come across" | it works from every earlier build | the builds actually tested |

Expert opinion reads as fact: a company that writes as the authority in its
field cannot turn a claim into an opinion with "we think".

## Quotations are our claims

A sentence from a customer that we publish becomes our claim. Beside it, show
the date, the product version it refers to and the source. Disclose any
connection: employment, partnership, a free license, a pre-release build
("tested a pre-release build supplied by Fontlab Ltd."). Retire a quotation
about a version the speaker has not used. Offer nothing in return for a
quotable sentence.

## "Free", earlier prices, comparisons

- "Free" carries its condition in the same unit everywhere: subject line,
  preheader, post, notification, translation. "Free if you bought TransType 4
  in 2026" passes; "TransType 5 is free for you" to a mixed list fails. If the condition does not fit, the word does not go out.
- A "was" price or a "later" price is one that was or will actually be charged.
- A crossgrade or upgrade rule appears in copy only once the store applies it.
- In a comparison table every cell states what that maker publishes, with
  source and date read; where a maker states nothing, the cell reads "not
  stated on `[page]`, read `[date]`" in the same type size. Like is compared
  with like (a time-limited full license and a reduced app go in separate rows);
  prices in different currencies carry a note in the row. Names only, no
  competitor logos. A comparison is rechecked whenever a competitor changes.

## Disclosure in partner, creator and founder posts

The reader learns the relationship before the recommendation, in their
language, where the recommendation is read.

- Video: say it and show it on screen; a description field alone is not enough.
- German readers: "Werbung" or "Anzeige" at the start of the post. A sponsored
  segment of ninety seconds or more is announced as "Dauerwerbesendung" at the
  start. Affiliate links are labelled on the whole post, on each link, or in a
  note before the post. Mark German wording `[NATIVE REVIEW]`.
- A founder or employee posting about the company's products names the role in
  the post and writes private views as "I", not "we".
- A business profile used by a person needs an Impressum link for German
  readers; flag it, do not draft legal text.
- Affiliate invitations state terms only, never what a partner might earn.

## A test on buyers

When a brief asks for variants to test, deliver them as a test, not as a
choice of favourites:

1. One variable per test, in this order of usual effect: the promise
   (headline or subject line), the price or terms, any bonus, the format.
2. Two variants making different supported promises; body, offer and trial
   identical.
3. A key per variant (a code or a tracked link) that reaches the store's
   records; mark `[CONFIRM DESTINATION: tracking]` if none is supplied.
4. The verdict is purchases or revenue per recipient over a stated window;
   clicks per delivered address decide when purchases are too few. Opens are
   indicative only (privacy features inflate them); never resend to
   "non-openers". Report group sizes, and call a difference that could be chance "no
   detectable difference".
5. The winner becomes the version to beat; it is not rewritten on a schedule.

Never write a variant that the evidence does not support because a test will
"settle it". A test chooses between true promises.

## Rejected

| Draft or tactic | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "If you don't act now, you'll regret it" | Guilt and fear as the reason to act |
| "Your colleagues will wonder how you did it" | Status imagery; invented reader feelings |
| An order form for a product that is not ready | A dry test; use a waitlist that says what it is |
| An advertisement styled as a magazine article | Editorial disguise |
| "Faster than ever, more precise than anyone" | A claim enlarged to beat a rival's |
| Resending the same email to non-openers and calling it a test | Not a controlled test, and a deliverability risk |
| "#ad" at the end of a long caption | Disclosure placed where it is not read |
| A price rise announced without its date and new price | Invented urgency by omission |
| "Leave a review and get 20% off" | Rewarded review: advertising, and a distorted rating |
| "Most designers forget to back up their masters" | Negative norm: teaches the behavior it names |
| "Our team will get back to you" when one person answers | Implied staff |
| A stock photo beside a real customer's quotation | Misattributes the person |
| "Our AI personas told us designers want..." | A model's output cited as research |
| A generated image presented as the product's output | False demonstration |
| "Only a few left", "limited quantity", "we are the number one" | Scarcity and rank claims that software cannot support |
| "I know how defeated I felt"; "You picked a special day to stop by" | Invented feelings; manufactured intrigue |
| Coupons only for readers a tool marked price-sensitive; "one-time offer" downsells | Hidden personal pricing; pressure |
| A testimonial drafted for the customer to sign | Not the customer's words |
| Competitor names in tags; "best, top-rated" in metadata | Misleading metadata |

## Words that need a fact

In a count of more than seven hundred thousand press releases, "innovate",
"unique", "leading provider", "world class" and "cost-effective" were among the
most frequent words, and editors named "leading", "we're excited about" and
"solutions" as the most overused. Each one in a draft is a prompt to supply the
specific fact it stands in for, or to delete it.

## Numbers not to quote

Do not repeat figures that circulate without a traceable source: the Netflix
recommendation billion, Amazon's 35 percent from recommendations, the "rule of
seven", "95 percent of decisions are subconscious". Mark any such number
`[VERIFY CLAIM: primary source]` or remove the sentence's number.
