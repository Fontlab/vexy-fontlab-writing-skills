# Chinese (Traditional) terms

Portable term table, snapshot of 01 October 2026, generated from the core translation memory `localization/zh-hant-core.tmx` of the FontLab writing guide by `tools/scripts/export_terms.py`. 54 of 286 terms are approved or protected; a proposed translation is a candidate for a native reviewer, and the English term is the fallback until it is approved. Use newer supplied project data when it disagrees with this snapshot.

A status of do-not-translate protects the English spelling; it does not approve a draft English name. The definition says what the term means in FontLab; the note says why the translation was chosen or how to inflect it. The fallback original term is a plain English phrase to translate instead of the term when the term itself will not travel (stem: main stroke; overshoot: optical surplus).

| on-curve point | point on the curve | 曲線上點 | proposed | FontForge zh_TW has 曲線上點. | An on-curve point lies on the outline itself and is where two segments meet. In FontLab it is a node; the term is used when the contrast with off-curve points matters, for example when a TrueType outline stores both kinds in one list. You place on-curve points at extremes so hinting and interpolation have stable anchors. |

| stroke |  | 筆畫 | proposed | Adobe zh-TW (填色與筆畫), Glyphs 3 zh-Hant (建立為筆畫) and FontForge zh_TW (展開筆畫) use 筆畫 for a stroke applied to a path. | tonal map | darkness map | 色調圖 | proposed | No attestation; 色調 is Adobe zh-TW's word for tone. | A tonal map is the reading of brightness across a source image that a fill uses to decide where to put geometry. Dark areas widen a strip or crowd a pattern; light areas narrow or thin it. You control the mapping with the Brightness, Contrast, and Source Levels rows, which change what counts as dark before any geometry is drawn. Adjust the map rather than the fill when the result is uniformly too heavy or too sparse. |
| Tunni line |  | Tunni 線 | proposed | Eponym kept in Latin script; 線 for line. | A Tunni line joins the two handles of a curve segment, with a control on it that Vextra draws as a pill. Drag the control and both handles move in proportion, so the depth of the curve changes without its balance changing. You use it when a curve is the right shape but the wrong fullness, which is the correction that is slowest to make one handle at a time. Named after Eduardo Tunni, so keep the capital T. |
