# FontLab and Vexy terms

Portable glossary snapshot: 13 September 2026.

Start with the entry's status and product scope, then follow its definition
into the sentence you are checking. Keep the term stable while the explanation
around it develops. A collision note identifies where a familiar noun changes
meaning between applications; use that distinction before choosing a correction.

Approved house usage does not establish current availability. Draft decisions
remain open, and deprecated names may belong in history or exact quotations.
This catalog is not an exhaustive list of valid words, products or domains.
Use applicable newer evidence when supplied and state the scope of any disagreement.

Definitions and collision notes come from the writing guide's glossary. Research
paths remain in the source repository. Usage examples are omitted so a naming
check does not turn an old style preference into a factual prohibition. For
worked definitions and editing decisions, see [the worked cases](moves.md).

## Type design

<span id="ascender"></span>

### ascender

Id: `ascender`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

An ascender is the part of a lowercase letter that extends above the
x-height, as in b or d. Ascender height describes the intended alignment of
these parts and may differ from cap height. Do not confuse this design
measurement with similarly named vertical metrics in exported font tables.
Line spacing depends on those fields and on the application using them.

Related: [descender](#descender), [cap height](#cap-height), [x-height](#x-height), [OS/2 table](#os2-table).

<span id="baseline"></span>

### baseline

Id: `baseline`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

A baseline is a reference line used to align text. In conventional
horizontal Latin font design, the baseline is at y = 0, with ascenders above
it and descenders below it. Other scripts and layout systems can use
different baseline conventions. Identify the relevant baseline when
discussing alignment across scripts or vertical text.

Related: [x-height](#x-height), [cap height](#cap-height), [ascender](#ascender), [UPM](#upm).

<span id="bowl"></span>

### bowl

Id: `bowl`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A bowl is a rounded stroke that encloses a counter, as in b, d or p. Its
shape, thickness and junctions affect the letter's balance. The appropriate
number of curve segments depends on the design. Nodes at extrema can help
editing and hinting, but a fixed segment count does not guarantee compatible
masters or good interpolation.

Related: [counter](#counter), [stem](#stem), [overshoot](#overshoot), [extremum](#extremum).

<span id="cap-height"></span>

### cap height

Id: `cap-height`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Cap height is the distance from the baseline to the top of a flat capital
such as H. Rounded or pointed capitals may extend beyond it through
overshoot. Record the intended value in Font Info. Cap height describes a
design measurement; alignment zones and line-spacing metrics are separate
settings, even when their values are related.

Related: [ascender](#ascender), [baseline](#baseline), [x-height](#x-height), [alignment zone](#alignment-zone).

<span id="character"></span>

### character

Id: `character`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A character is an abstract unit of written text, such as a letter or
punctuation mark. A glyph is its visual representation. The relationship is
not one to one: several characters can form a ligature, and a character can
have several glyph forms. A user-perceived character can also consist of
several Unicode codepoints. Keep characters, codepoints and glyphs distinct
when discussing encoding or text processing.

Related: [glyph](#glyph), [Unicode codepoint](#unicode-codepoint), [cmap](#cmap), [Private Use Area](#private-use-area).

<span id="contour"></span>

### contour

Id: `contour`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra, vexy-lines.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: outline, path.

A contour is an open or closed sequence of connected line and curve
segments. Its nodes and control points describe its geometry. One glyph or
vector object can contain several contours. A counter is an area of space,
not a synonym for a contour. When explaining fill behaviour, identify the
outline format and fill rule before prescribing a contour direction.

Related: [node](#node), [segment](#segment), [winding direction](#winding-direction), [counter](#counter).

<span id="counter"></span>

### counter

Id: `counter`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A counter is the enclosed or partly enclosed space within a letterform, such
as the enclosed space in an o or the open space in a c. The surrounding
strokes define it. A closed counter may have an inner contour as its
boundary; an open counter does not require a separate contour. Judge its
size and shape together with the surrounding strokes.

Related: [contour](#contour), [winding direction](#winding-direction), [stem](#stem), [bowl](#bowl).

<span id="descender"></span>

### descender

Id: `descender`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

A descender is the part of a letter that extends below the baseline, as in p
or y. Its design height is usually recorded as a negative coordinate
relative to the baseline. Exported descender metrics are separate fields
used by applications for layout. Whether a deep glyph clips depends on the
relevant metrics, application and text container, not on one design value
alone.

Related: [ascender](#ascender), [baseline](#baseline), [OS/2 table](#os2-table), [UPM](#upm).

<span id="extremum"></span>

### extremum

Id: `extremum`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: extreme point, node at extreme.

An extremum is a point where a curve reaches a local leftmost, rightmost,
topmost or bottommost position. The plural is extrema. Placing nodes at
extrema can help editing and hinting. If you add nodes to masters intended
for interpolation, preserve the correspondence between their contours and
points. Different extremum positions alone do not make masters incompatible.

Related: [node](#node), [contour](#contour), [master compatibility](#master-compatibility), [hinting](#hinting).

<span id="glyph"></span>

### glyph

Id: `glyph`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

A glyph is one drawn shape in a font: a letter, a figure, a mark, or a piece of a
composite. A character is what a reader types and a glyph is what a renderer draws,
and one character can map to several glyphs while several characters can map to
one. You edit a glyph in the Glyph window, where its contours, elements, anchors,
and metrics all live together. The distinction matters in any sentence about
encoding, since a font maps codepoints to glyphs through the cmap table.

Related: [character](#character), [contour](#contour), [glyph name](#glyph-name), [Unicode codepoint](#unicode-codepoint).

<span id="handle"></span>

### handle

Id: `handle`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: control point, BCP.

A handle is an off-curve control point used to shape a curve segment. A
cubic Bézier segment has two control points; a quadratic segment has one.
Moving a handle changes the curve without moving its endpoints. In tools
that display handle arms, the arm connects a control point to an endpoint.
Name the curve type when the number or behaviour of handles matters.

Related: [node](#node), [segment](#segment), [Tunni line](#tunni-line), [G2 continuity](#g2-continuity).

<span id="node"></span>

### node

Id: `node`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: point.

A node is a point on a contour where segments meet, and its type decides how they
meet. A sharp node lets two segments arrive at any angle. A smooth node keeps the
handles collinear so the curve passes through without a kink. A tangent node joins
a line to a curve smoothly. A G2 node matches curvature as well as direction. You
select a node and drag it, or nudge it, and the segments on both sides follow. Put
nodes at the extremes so hinting and interpolation have something to hold.

Related: [handle](#handle), [segment](#segment), [contour](#contour), [extremum](#extremum).

<span id="overshoot"></span>

### overshoot

Id: `overshoot`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Overshoot is the extension of a rounded or pointed shape beyond an alignment
line so that it appears as tall as a flat shape. For example, an o may
extend above the x-height and below the baseline. Its amount depends on the
design and intended size. PostScript alignment zones can describe these
overshoot regions for hinting; there is no universal percentage to apply.

Related: [alignment zone](#alignment-zone), [x-height](#x-height), [baseline](#baseline), [bowl](#bowl).

<span id="segment"></span>

### segment

Id: `segment`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: curve segment.

A segment is the portion of a contour between two on-curve endpoints. It may
be a straight line or a curve. A quadratic Bézier segment has one off-curve
control point; a cubic segment has two. Describe the segment type and the
relevant editing command rather than assuming that every curve has two
handles or that changing a node type performs every conversion.

Related: [node](#node), [handle](#handle), [contour](#contour), [extremum](#extremum).

<span id="serif"></span>

### serif

Id: `serif`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A serif is the finishing stroke at the end of a stem or arm. Its shape sorts a
face into the familiar classes: oldstyle, transitional, Didone, slab. You draw a
serif once and reuse it as a component or an element, which keeps a family
consistent and makes a late change cheap. Serifs are small, so they take the most
damage at low resolution, which is where hinting and careful alignment zones earn
their place.

Related: [terminal](#terminal), [element](#element), [component](#component), [hinting](#hinting).

<span id="stem"></span>

### stem

Id: `stem`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

A stem is a main stroke of a letter, usually the vertical one: the two uprights of
an H, the single upright of an l. Stem width sets the weight of a face, and stems
that disagree across a family break its rhythm. You measure a stem with the
Measurement tool and record the standard widths in Font Info, where hinting and
the stem links read them. Vextra reuses the word for the paired edges it detects
in general artwork, not only in letters.

Related: [counter](#counter), [stem link](#stem-link), [hinting](#hinting), [alignment zone](#alignment-zone).

<span id="terminal"></span>

### terminal

Id: `terminal`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A terminal is the end of a stroke without a serif, such as the ending of a c
or f. Its shape may be rounded, angled or cut off, depending on the design.
Compare related terminals across a typeface at the intended reading size.
The anatomical term does not prescribe how to construct or store the shape
in an editor.

Related: [serif](#serif), [stem](#stem), [contour](#contour), [overshoot](#overshoot).

<span id="upm"></span>

### UPM

Id: `upm`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: no.

Also written: units per em, unitsPerEm.

UPM, units per em, specifies how many font units make up one em. It sets the
scale for outline coordinates and metrics when a renderer converts them to a
text size. Values such as 1000 and 2048 are common; TrueType outlines do not
require a power of two. When changing UPM, distinguish changing the scale
value from rescaling the outlines and metrics.

Related: [baseline](#baseline), [advance width](#advance-width), [hinting](#hinting), [OTF](#otf).

<span id="winding-direction"></span>

### winding direction

Id: `winding-direction`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: contour direction, CW, CCW.

Winding direction is the order in which a contour runs, clockwise or
counter-clockwise, and it decides which areas the renderer fills. FontLab follows
the PostScript convention: outer contours wind counter-clockwise and counters wind
clockwise. The letter O is two contours, one each way. You check direction before
you export, and reverse a contour when a counter fills solid or an outer shape
turns hollow. A renderer applies the rule at draw time, so the error shows in the
proof rather than in the editor.

Related: [contour](#contour), [counter](#counter), [Remove Overlap](#remove-overlap), [OTF](#otf).

<span id="x-height"></span>

### x-height

Id: `x-height`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

The x-height is the height of a lowercase x above the baseline, and it stands for
the height of the lowercase in general. It does more for apparent size than the
point size does: two faces at the same size look different when their x-heights
differ. You record it in Font Info so hinting and alignment zones can use it, and
you draw round letters slightly above it to allow for overshoot. Write it
lowercase with a hyphen, even at the start of a sentence recast to avoid one.

Related: [baseline](#baseline), [cap height](#cap-height), [overshoot](#overshoot), [alignment zone](#alignment-zone).

## Font engineering

<span id="advance-width"></span>

### advance width

Id: `advance-width`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: advance, width.

Advance width is the horizontal distance allocated to a glyph before layout
adjustments, measured in font units. In an OpenType font, the base values
are stored in the `hmtx` table. Kerning and other positioning operations can
adjust advances during layout without rewriting those base values. Advance
width differs from the visible outline width; a space can have an advance
without an outline.

Related: [sidebearing](#sidebearing), [metrics](#metrics), [kerning](#kerning), [UPM](#upm).

<span id="alignment-zone"></span>

### alignment zone

Id: `alignment-zone`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: blue zone, zone.

An alignment zone is a region around a common vertical alignment, such as
the baseline or cap height, used in PostScript hinting. It can include the
overshoot of rounded letters. A rasterizer uses zone information when
fitting outlines to a pixel grid. Keep this mechanism distinct from design
measurements and from the control-value and instruction mechanisms used in
TrueType hinting.

Related: [hinting](#hinting), [autohinting](#autohinting), [overshoot](#overshoot), [x-height](#x-height).

<span id="anchor"></span>

### anchor

Id: `anchor`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

An anchor is a named attachment point in a glyph that says where a mark belongs.
A base letter carries a top anchor, and the accent carries a matching one, so the
accent lands in the same relation on every letter that uses it. You place anchors
in the Anchors and Pins panel, and FontLab uses them both to build composites and
to generate mark attachment lookups in GPOS, which is what positions marks in text
the font never composed itself.

Related: [mark attachment](#mark-attachment), [component](#component), [composite glyph](#composite-glyph), [GPOS](#gpos).

<span id="autohinting"></span>

### autohinting

Id: `autohinting`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Autohinting generates hints for a font from its outlines and its declared stems
and zones, in one pass over the glyphs. It is a starting point rather than a
finish: it hints the regular cases well and leaves the awkward glyphs for you.
You run it after the alignment zones and standard stem widths are correct in Font
Info, because it reads them. Then you check the glyphs that matter at text sizes
and correct by hand what the pass got wrong.

Related: [hinting](#hinting), [alignment zone](#alignment-zone), [stem link](#stem-link), [CVT table](#cvt-table).

<span id="axis"></span>

### axis

Id: `axis`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: design axis.

An axis is one dimension of font variation, such as weight, width or optical
size. In an OpenType variable font, each axis has a four-character tag and a
minimum, default and maximum value. Registered tags, such as `wght`, have
defined meanings. Preserve tag spelling and case, and check the axis
registry when assigning a custom tag.

Related: [design space](#design-space), [master](#master), [instance](#instance), [variable font](#variable-font).

<span id="component"></span>

### component

Id: `component`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A component is a reference to another glyph placed inside this one, so that
aacute holds the a and the acute rather than copies of their outlines. Change the
source glyph and every glyph that references it follows. You build composites from
components, position them with anchors, and keep them as references through to
export, where a TrueType flavoured font stores them in the glyf table. In FontLab
a component is one kind of element reference.

Related: [composite glyph](#composite-glyph), [element](#element), [element reference](#element-reference), [anchor](#anchor).

<span id="composite-glyph"></span>

### composite glyph

Id: `composite-glyph`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: composite.

A composite glyph is built from references to other glyphs rather than from its
own outlines: an accented letter, a currency symbol with a bar, a stacked
fraction. You assemble it from components and anchor them to the base, so a change
to the base or to the mark reaches every composite that uses it. Composites keep a
large accented character set consistent and small. A composite that has been
decomposed is no longer one, and no longer follows its sources.

Related: [component](#component), [anchor](#anchor), [element reference](#element-reference), [glyph](#glyph).

<span id="conversion-profile"></span>

### conversion profile

Id: `conversion-profile`. Status: approved. Applies to: transtype.

Translatable: yes.

A conversion profile is the saved set of choices TransType applies to a
conversion: the output format, the naming scheme, the filters, and the options
that go with them. You build a profile once for a job you repeat, then run it over
a new set of sources without repeating the decisions. It matters because a family
converted with two different sets of options ships as two families that do not
quite match, which shows up in a font menu rather than in a test.

Related: [TransType 5](#transtype-5), [font target](#font-target), [typographic family name](#typographic-family-name), [OTF](#otf).

<span id="cvt-table"></span>

### CVT table

Id: `cvt-table`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

Also written: cvt, control value table.

The CVT table holds the control values that TrueType hinting instructions refer
to: the stem widths, heights, and other measurements a font wants held constant
across glyphs. An instruction cites a CVT entry rather than a raw number, so one
change to the table reaches every glyph that cites it. You work with it through
the TrueType Hinting panel. It ships in the font, alongside the instructions
themselves and the prep and fpgm programs that set the machine up.

Related: [hinting](#hinting), [stem link](#stem-link), [TTF](#ttf), [autohinting](#autohinting).

<span id="design-space"></span>

### design space

Id: `design-space`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

A design space describes the positions defined by a font project's variation
axes. Masters provide source data at specified locations; instances
represent results at selected locations. User coordinates and internal
design coordinates may be connected by a mapping, or may have the same
values. State which coordinate system a number belongs to when the
distinction affects a procedure.

Related: [axis](#axis), [master](#master), [instance](#instance), [interpolation](#interpolation).

<span id="element"></span>

### element

Id: `element`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

An element is FontLab's container for a piece of drawing inside a glyph: one or
more contours, an image, or a reference to another element. A glyph holds
elements, and an element holds contours, which is the layer of structure that
makes a serif or a stem reusable across a family. You work with elements in the
Elements panel and with the Element tool. The word is FontLab's own; other editors
call the nearest thing a path group or a component.

Related: [element reference](#element-reference), [component](#component), [contour](#contour), [glyph](#glyph).

<span id="element-reference"></span>

### element reference

Id: `element-reference`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

An element reference is a placed copy of an element that still points at the
original. Edit the original and every reference updates. You use references to
share a serif, a stem, or a whole letter across a family, and you decompose a
reference only when a glyph genuinely needs to diverge. A component is the
familiar case of an element reference: one glyph placed inside another. Keep
references intact through the design phase, since decomposing early throws away
the link that makes a late change cheap.

Related: [element](#element), [component](#component), [composite glyph](#composite-glyph), [glyph](#glyph).

<span id="font-target"></span>

### font target

Id: `font-target`. Status: approved. Applies to: transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: target.

A font target is what TransType produces from a source: the output font, with its
format, its names, and the filters applied on the way. Sources go in on one side
and targets come out on the other, and one source can produce several targets. You
set the target options before you convert, and you name the target in a procedure
step so a reader knows which side of the conversion a setting belongs to.

Related: [TransType 5](#transtype-5), [conversion profile](#conversion-profile), [OTF](#otf), [TTC](#ttc).

<span id="hinting"></span>

### hinting

Id: `hinting`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Hinting is the extra information a font carries so that its outlines land
predictably on a pixel grid at small sizes. PostScript hinting declares stems and
alignment zones and leaves the rasterizer to interpret them. TrueType hinting
gives instructions that move points explicitly, with the control values in the
CVT table. You work on PostScript hints in the glyph and in Font Info, and on
TrueType hints with the TrueType Hinting tool and panel. Autohinting produces a
starting point you then correct.

Related: [autohinting](#autohinting), [alignment zone](#alignment-zone), [stem link](#stem-link), [CVT table](#cvt-table).

<span id="instance"></span>

### instance

Id: `instance`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

An instance is a particular result at a location in a font's variation
space. A named instance, such as Semibold, gives a selected location a name
that applications can present. Other locations need not have names. In
FontLab, configured instances can be exported as static fonts or included as
named instances in a variable font.

Related: [master](#master), [axis](#axis), [design space](#design-space), [variable font](#variable-font).

<span id="interpolation"></span>

### interpolation

Id: `interpolation`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Interpolation calculates values between source values. In font design, it
can calculate outlines, metrics and other data from masters at specified
design-space locations. Outline interpolation requires corresponding contour
and point structures. Structural compatibility makes the calculation
possible, but does not guarantee a good drawing: inspect intermediate
results as well as the masters. Extrapolation calculates beyond the source
range.

Related: [master](#master), [master compatibility](#master-compatibility), [instance](#instance), [variable font](#variable-font).

<span id="kern-exception"></span>

### kern exception

Id: `kern-exception`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

A kern exception is a pair value that overrides what the classes would otherwise
apply. One member of a class rarely behaves like the rest, and the exception says
so without breaking the class apart. You create one in the Kerning panel by
kerning the single pair while its classes stay in place. Keep exceptions few: a
class with many exceptions is the wrong class, and the cheaper fix is to split it
rather than to list every disagreement.

Related: [kerning class](#kerning-class), [kerning pair](#kerning-pair), [kerning](#kerning), [GPOS](#gpos).

<span id="kerning"></span>

### kerning

Id: `kerning`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Kerning adjusts the spacing of particular glyph combinations beyond their
normal advances. A pair such as AV may need a different gap from the one
produced by its unadjusted metrics. OpenType fonts commonly store horizontal
kerning in `GPOS` lookups associated with the `kern` feature; some formats
and workflows use a separate `kern` table. Name the mechanism when it
matters to export or compatibility.

Related: [kerning pair](#kerning-pair), [kerning class](#kerning-class), [visual kerning](#visual-kerning), [GPOS](#gpos).

<span id="kerning-class"></span>

### kerning class

Id: `kerning-class`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: class kerning.

A kerning class groups glyphs that need the same kerning. You put every glyph that
behaves alike on one side into one class, then kern the class once instead of
kerning each pair inside it. FontLab applies the class value to every member pair
and keeps single pairs as exceptions where a member needs its own value. Classes
matter because a text face carries thousands of readable pairs, and a hand kerned
list of that size is unmaintainable. FontLab writes the result into GPOS as class
based positioning.

Related: [kerning](#kerning), [kerning pair](#kerning-pair), [kern exception](#kern-exception), [GPOS](#gpos).

<span id="kerning-pair"></span>

### kerning pair

Id: `kerning-pair`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A kerning pair associates two glyphs, or two kerning classes, with a spacing
adjustment. In horizontal kerning, a negative value normally reduces the gap
and a positive value increases it. An individual glyph pair may provide an
exception to class kerning, but it can also exist without a class rule.
Check pairs in representative text as well as in isolation.

Related: [kerning](#kerning), [kerning class](#kerning-class), [kern exception](#kern-exception), [GPOS](#gpos).

<span id="mark-attachment"></span>

### mark attachment

Id: `mark-attachment`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Mark attachment positions a combining mark relative to a base glyph, a
ligature component or another mark. OpenType `GPOS` lookups describe the
anchor relationships, and the shaping engine applies the positioning during
layout. FontLab can generate attachment code from suitable anchors. Check
the relevant generation settings and test combinations, including stacked
marks, rather than assuming that every anchor automatically becomes a
lookup.

Related: [anchor](#anchor), [GPOS](#gpos), [lookup](#lookup), [composite glyph](#composite-glyph).

<span id="master"></span>

### master

Id: `master`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A master provides source outlines and other font data at a specified
location in a design space. Masters can describe weight, width or other
variations, including intermediate locations. FontLab uses corresponding
data from masters to calculate instances. For outline interpolation, check
contour and point compatibility in each affected glyph; the word master
alone does not guarantee that every glyph is present or compatible.

Related: [instance](#instance), [axis](#axis), [interpolation](#interpolation), [master compatibility](#master-compatibility).

<span id="master-compatibility"></span>

### master compatibility

Id: `master-compatibility`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Master compatibility is the condition every glyph must meet before it can
interpolate: the same number of contours in the same order, with the same number
of nodes and the same start point in each. FontLab reports incompatible glyphs and
gives you Matchmaker to repair them by matching contours and start points between
masters. Fix compatibility before you tune shapes, because a repair that reorders
nodes moves every curve that depends on them.

Related: [master](#master), [interpolation](#interpolation), [Matchmaker](#matchmaker), [extremum](#extremum).

<span id="metrics"></span>

### metrics

Id: `metrics`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Metrics are the measurements that place a glyph in a line: its advance width and
its two sidebearings. Vertical metrics are a separate set, the font wide values
for ascender, descender, and line gap. You work on horizontal metrics in the
Metrics panel and with the Metrics tool, and on vertical metrics in Font Info.
Say which set you mean whenever a page discusses both, because a reader who
applies a horizontal instruction to the vertical fields breaks line spacing
everywhere the font is used.

Related: [advance width](#advance-width), [sidebearing](#sidebearing), [ascender](#ascender), [kerning](#kerning).

<span id="sidebearing"></span>

### sidebearing

Id: `sidebearing`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: LSB, RSB, side bearing.

A sidebearing is the space between a glyph's outline and the edge of its advance
width: the left sidebearing before the shape, the right sidebearing after it.
Together with the advance width it sets how the glyph spaces against every other
glyph, which is the work that carries a text face further than kerning does. You
edit sidebearings in the Metrics panel or with the Metrics tool, one glyph at a
time or across a selection, and you check them in a word rather than in isolation.

Related: [advance width](#advance-width), [metrics](#metrics), [kerning](#kerning), [tracking](#tracking).

<span id="stem-link"></span>

### stem link

Id: `stem-link`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: stem hint.

A stem link ties two edges of a stroke together as a stem, so hinting can hold
their distance as the size changes. A vertical link governs a horizontal
measurement and a horizontal link governs a vertical one. You add links in the
glyph, either by hand or from an autohinting pass, and you keep them consistent
with the standard stem widths declared in Font Info. Vextra reuses the term for
the paired edges it detects in artwork that is not type.

Related: [hinting](#hinting), [autohinting](#autohinting), [stem](#stem), [CVT table](#cvt-table).

<span id="styling-group-name"></span>

### styling group name

Id: `styling-group-name`. Status: approved. Applies to: transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: SGN, styling link value, SLV, styling link name, SLN.

The styling group name, SGN, ties together the styles an application reaches
through its bold and italic buttons, and the styling link value and styling link
name say which slot each style takes in that group. The mechanism is older than
typographic family names and narrower: four slots, no more. You set these fields
in TransType when a family has to behave correctly in applications that still
drive style selection from those buttons.

Related: [typographic family name](#typographic-family-name), [Groups](#groups), [TransType 5](#transtype-5), [conversion profile](#conversion-profile).

<span id="tracking"></span>

### tracking

Id: `tracking`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, transtype.

Translatable: yes.

Tracking changes the spacing across a run of text, rather than correcting
particular pairs as kerning does. Layout applications apply it to text.
TransType's Tracking filter changes spacing in the font data itself. That
changes the font's starting metrics; a layout application can still apply
additional tracking to text set in the resulting font.

Related: [kerning](#kerning), [sidebearing](#sidebearing), [metrics](#metrics), [TransType 5](#transtype-5).

<span id="typographic-family-name"></span>

### typographic family name

Id: `typographic-family-name`. Status: approved. Applies to: transtype, fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: TFN, typographic style name, TSN.

The typographic family name, TFN, is the name that groups every style of a family
under one entry, and the typographic style name, TSN, is what distinguishes a
style inside it. They sit beside the older four style family and style names that
legacy applications read. You set them in TransType and in Font Info, and you keep
them consistent across a family, because an app that reads these fields builds its
font menu from them.

Related: [styling group name](#styling-group-name), [TransType 5](#transtype-5), [OS/2 table](#os2-table), [font target](#font-target).

<span id="variable-font"></span>

### variable font

Id: `variable-font`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A variable font contains data for design variation along one or more axes.
Applications select axis values and calculate the corresponding outlines and
other variable data. Named instances identify selected locations, but users
are not limited to those locations when the application exposes axis
controls. A variable font's supported range depends on its data; it need not
contain every style in the family.

Related: [axis](#axis), [master](#master), [instance](#instance), [design space](#design-space).

<span id="visual-kerning"></span>

### visual kerning

Id: `visual-kerning`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Visual kerning is kerning that FontLab proposes by measuring the shapes, so that a
font gets a usable first pass without a person typing thousands of values. You run
it over a selection, then review what it produced, because a measurement of two
outlines does not know about the words a reader will meet. Treat the result as a
draft: keep the pairs it got right, correct the ones it did not, and kern the
difficult pairs by eye in real words.

Related: [kerning](#kerning), [kerning pair](#kerning-pair), [kerning class](#kerning-class), [FontLab 8](#fontlab-8).

<span id="adobe-glyph-list"></span>

### Adobe Glyph List

Id: `adobe-glyph-list`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

Also written: AGL.

The Adobe Glyph List maps standard glyph names to Unicode codepoints, so that a
glyph named aacute is understood as U plus 00E1 by any tool that follows it. You
name glyphs by it wherever it covers the character, because tools and text engines
can then infer the mapping without being told twice. Where it does not cover a
glyph, you fall back to a consistent scheme of your own and assign codepoints
explicitly. Write the name in full on first mention, then AGL.

<span id="cmap"></span>

### cmap

Id: `cmap`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, transtype.

Translatable: no.

Also written: cmap table, cmap subtable.

The `cmap` table maps character codes to glyph indices. Its Unicode
subtables provide the initial mapping used in text rendering; other
subtables support particular encodings or variation sequences. FontLab
generates these mappings from the project's encoding data. A glyph without
its own Unicode assignment can still appear in typed text through
substitution, such as a ligature or alternate selected by the shaping
engine.

### color font table

Id: `color-font-table`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: COLR, CPAL, sbix, CBDT, CBLC.

Colour-font tables store data for colour glyph rendering. Formats include
`COLR` with `CPAL`, SVG, `sbix`, and `CBDT` with `CBLC`. They differ in how
they describe vector or bitmap content and in application support. Including
several formats can extend coverage, but does not guarantee rendering in
every application. Identify the exported formats and test the intended
targets.

<span id="contextual-alternates"></span>

### contextual alternates

Id: `contextual-alternates`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: calt.

Contextual alternates, the calt feature, cover substitutions an app makes because
of what sits around a glyph rather than because a reader asked. In a script face
the text engine replaces an entry stroke after a letter that already ends in one,
and in a face with repeating shapes it replaces the second of a pair. You write the rules with context in feature code, and apps
usually leave calt on by default, which makes it the feature most likely to
surprise you when a rule is too broad.

<span id="feature-code"></span>

### feature code

Id: `feature-code`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

Also written: FEA, feature file.

Feature code is the text syntax that describes OpenType features: classes,
lookups, substitution and positioning rules, grouped into features by tag. It is
usually called FEA, after the file extension. You edit it in the Features panel,
compile it to check for errors, and keep it under the same review as the drawings,
since a rule that fires on the wrong glyph is as visible as a wrong outline.
FontLab compiles it into GSUB and GPOS at export.

<span id="fractions"></span>

### fractions

Id: `fractions`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: frac.

Fractions are numerical forms such as 1/2. In OpenType, the `frac` feature
supports diagonal fraction composition through glyph substitution. A font
may provide precomposed fraction glyphs, numerator and denominator forms, or
both. The separate `numr` and `dnom` features provide numerator and
denominator forms. Do not substitute the `sups` and `subs` feature names for
these mechanisms or promise support for every input sequence without testing
it.

Related: [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [GSUB](#gsub), [tabular figures](#tabular-figures), [oldstyle figures](#oldstyle-figures).

<span id="glyph-index"></span>

### glyph index

Id: `glyph-index`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

Also written: Glyph ID, GID.

A glyph index is the number a glyph occupies in the font's own order, counting
from zero. Text engines work in indices after the cmap has done its job, and
binary tables reference glyphs by index rather than by name. You rarely set one
by hand, but you meet it when you inspect a compiled font or debug a lookup that
fires on the wrong glyph. The index depends on glyph order, so it changes when
the order does.

Related: [glyph name](#glyph-name), [cmap](#cmap), [lookup](#lookup), [Unicode codepoint](#unicode-codepoint).

<span id="glyph-name"></span>

### glyph name

Id: `glyph-name`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: no.

A glyph name identifies a glyph in an editing project, feature code or a
tool's data. Names such as a, a.sc and a.alt can distinguish related
drawings. They are separate from Unicode assignments and glyph indices, and
need not survive in every exported format. After renaming a glyph, check
references in feature code, classes and scripts, including references the
editor cannot update automatically.

Related: [glyph](#glyph), [Adobe Glyph List](#adobe-glyph-list), [glyph index](#glyph-index), [feature code](#feature-code).

<span id="gpos"></span>

### GPOS

Id: `gpos`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

GPOS is the OpenType table that holds positioning: kerning, mark attachment,
cursive attachment, and any rule that moves a glyph without replacing it. FontLab
writes your kerning here, as pairs and as class based positioning, and builds the
mark lookups from your anchors. You reach it through feature code rather than by
editing the table. A font may also carry a legacy kern table; modern text engines
read GPOS and many ignore the other.

Related: [GSUB](#gsub), [kerning](#kerning), [mark attachment](#mark-attachment), [lookup](#lookup).

<span id="gsub"></span>

### GSUB

Id: `gsub`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

`GSUB` is the OpenType table containing glyph-substitution lookups. It
supports operations such as replacing a glyph with an alternate, forming a
ligature or selecting a form from its context. FontLab compiles feature code
into these structures. To diagnose a missing substitution, check the glyphs,
compiled rules, feature selection, language and shaping order in the target
application.

Related: [GPOS](#gpos), [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [lookup](#lookup), [feature code](#feature-code).

<span id="ligature"></span>

### ligature

Id: `ligature`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

A ligature is a glyph used to represent a sequence of characters, such as
fi. OpenType substitution can replace several glyphs with one ligature
glyph. The `liga` and `dlig` features distinguish standard and discretionary
ligatures. Shaping does not change the underlying text; whether a later
document export preserves searchable text also depends on that export and
its text mappings.

Related: [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [GSUB](#gsub), [glyph name](#glyph-name), [stylistic set](#stylistic-set).

<span id="localized-forms"></span>

### localized forms

Id: `localized-forms`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: locl.

Localized forms are glyph variants selected for a text run's language. The
OpenType `locl` feature can provide these substitutions, for example
Bulgarian forms of Cyrillic letters. The shaping engine needs the relevant
language information, which the application may obtain from a setting or
other context. The substitution changes glyph selection, not the underlying
characters or their codepoints.

Related: [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [GSUB](#gsub), [lookup](#lookup), [Unicode codepoint](#unicode-codepoint).

<span id="lookup"></span>

### lookup

Id: `lookup`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

A lookup groups substitution or positioning rules of one type in `GSUB` or
`GPOS`. Features reference lookups, and several features can share one. The
shaping engine selects features and may process them in script-specific
stages; within a stage, LookupList order matters. Source-code order alone
does not describe the complete shaping sequence. Inspect the compiled tables
and the target engine when diagnosing ordering problems.

Related: [GSUB](#gsub), [GPOS](#gpos), [feature code](#feature-code), [OpenType class](#opentype-class).

<span id="oldstyle-figures"></span>

### oldstyle figures

Id: `oldstyle-figures`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: onum, text figures.

Oldstyle figures are numerals with varying heights, often including forms
with ascenders and descenders. The OpenType `onum` feature selects them when
provided by the font. Figure height and figure width are separate choices:
oldstyle figures can be proportional or tabular. Use tabular widths when
numerals must align in columns, whether their forms are oldstyle or lining.

Related: [tabular figures](#tabular-figures), [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [GSUB](#gsub), [fractions](#fractions).

<span id="opentype-class"></span>

### OpenType class

Id: `opentype-class`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: glyph class, feature class.

An OpenType class is a named group of glyphs that feature code can address at
once, written with an at sign before the name. One rule over two classes replaces
a page of single rules, and one edit to a class reaches every rule that cites it.
You define classes in the Classes panel or in feature code, and you keep their
order stable, because a substitution between two classes pairs them by position.
Kerning classes are a separate mechanism with the same idea.

Related: [feature code](#feature-code), [lookup](#lookup), [kerning class](#kerning-class), [GSUB](#gsub).

<span id="opentype-feature"></span>

### OpenType feature

Id: `opentype-feature`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: feature.

An OpenType feature identifies typographic behaviour through a
four-character tag, such as `liga`, `smcp` or `ss01`. Feature records
reference substitution or positioning lookups. In FontLab, you can write
feature code and compile it into the font. Whether and when a feature is
applied depends on the shaping engine, script, language and application
settings; not every feature is a user-controlled switch.

Related: [feature code](#feature-code), [GSUB](#gsub), [GPOS](#gpos), [lookup](#lookup).

<span id="os2-table"></span>

### OS/2 table

Id: `os2-table`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, transtype.

Translatable: no.

The OS/2 table carries the font's metadata for applications: vertical metrics,
weight and width classes, embedding permissions, the panose values, and the
codepage and Unicode range bits that say what the font covers. You fill it from
Font Info rather than by hand, and you check it before release, because a wrong
weight class puts a font in the wrong slot in a menu and wrong vertical metrics
change line spacing in every browser. Write it with the slash: OS/2.

Related: [ascender](#ascender), [descender](#descender), [cmap](#cmap), [OTF](#otf).

<span id="private-use-area"></span>

### Private Use Area

Id: `private-use-area`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

Also written: PUA.

A private-use area is one of three Unicode blocks whose codepoints can
receive meanings by private agreement. Different fonts or systems may assign
different meanings to the same private-use codepoint. Use a documented
agreement when encoding custom symbols there. An alternate glyph reached
through a feature does not require its own private-use assignment.

Related: [Unicode codepoint](#unicode-codepoint), [cmap](#cmap), [glyph name](#glyph-name), [stylistic set](#stylistic-set).

<span id="small-caps"></span>

### small caps

Id: `small-caps`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: smcp, c2sc.

Small caps are capital shapes drawn at roughly lowercase height, with their own
weight and width rather than scaled capitals. Apps apply the smcp feature to put
them in place of lowercase letters, and c2sc to put them in place of capitals, so
a run set in both features becomes uniform. You draw them as separate glyphs, usually with a
dot sc suffix, and write both features. Scaled capitals look thin beside real
text, which is the reason to draw them rather than let an app fake them.

Related: [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [GSUB](#gsub), [ligature](#ligature), [stylistic set](#stylistic-set).

<span id="stylistic-set"></span>

### stylistic set

Id: `stylistic-set`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: ss01, ssXX.

A stylistic set is a numbered group of alternate glyphs a reader can switch on,
from ss01 through ss20. Each set holds alternates that belong together: a single
storey a with a matching g, a set of flat topped figures. You name each set in the
font so an app can show a readable label instead of a number, and you keep one
idea per set. A set that mixes unrelated alternates gives a reader nothing they
can decide about.

Related: [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [contextual alternates](#contextual-alternates), [GSUB](#gsub), [small caps](#small-caps).

<span id="tabular-figures"></span>

### tabular figures

Id: `tabular-figures`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: tnum.

Tabular figures all carry the same advance width, so columns of numbers line up
whatever digits they contain. Apps apply the tnum feature to put them in place of
proportional figures. You draw them as a separate set and give every one the same width,
centring the narrow digits inside it. They matter in any table, price list, or
running total, and they look loose in prose, which is why a font offers both sets
rather than choosing for the reader.

Related: [oldstyle figures](#oldstyle-figures), [advance width](#advance-width), [OpenType feature](#opentype-feature), [fractions](#fractions).

<span id="unicode-codepoint"></span>

### Unicode codepoint

Id: `unicode-codepoint`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer.

Translatable: no.

Also written: codepoint, code point.

A Unicode codepoint is an integer from U+0000 to U+10FFFF. Not every
codepoint has an assigned character. Write it with U+ and at least four
hexadecimal digits, as in U+0041 for A. In a font editor, a glyph's Unicode
assignment connects it to an encoded character; the exported `cmap` records
the relevant mapping.

Related: [character](#character), [cmap](#cmap), [glyph name](#glyph-name), [Private Use Area](#private-use-area).

### TTC

TTC is the usual filename extension for a TrueType font collection: several
fonts stored in one file. Collection members can share tables, reducing

### TTF

Translatable: no.

Also written: .ttf, TrueType.

TTF is the usual filename extension for a font with TrueType outlines, which
use quadratic curves. An OpenType TTF can contain layout features and
variation data. Exporting cubic source outlines to this flavour requires
curve conversion, which can change the point structure and approximate the
original geometry. Check the exported outlines at the intended sizes.

A mask holds a second drawing, and what it does with that drawing differs by

Matchmaker is a FontLab tool for examining and correcting correspondence
between glyph masters. It helps align contour order and start points for
interpolation. Matching structures does not guarantee that intermediate
outlines look right. Inspect the resulting interpolation; changing contour
order or a start point can change correspondence without changing the
outline's geometry at the master location.

Related: [master compatibility](#master-compatibility), [master](#master), [interpolation](#interpolation), [node](#node).

### panel

Id: `panel`. Status: approved. Applies to: all.

Translatable: yes.

A panel groups related controls or information within an application's
interface. It may be docked or floating, depending on the application. Use
its displayed name followed by panel in lowercase, as in the Kerning panel.
Distinguish panels, windows, dialogs and panes by their role and established
product terminology, not solely by whether they float, dock or contain tabs.

Related: [property bar](#property-bar), [dialog](#dialog), [Layers](#layers), [Transform](#transform).

<span id="pencil"></span>

### Pencil

Id: `pencil`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-lines, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

The Pencil tool draws freehand, and what it draws on differs by product. In
FontLab it converts a freehand gesture into curves and line segments without
asking you to think about node types. In Fontographer it is one of three outline
drawing tools, beside Pen and Rapid. In Vexy Lines it creates and edits Handmade
fills as vector lines. In Vexy Vextra it is the Correcting Pencil, which redraws
part of an existing path as readily as it starts a new one.

**Shared term:** FontLab and Fontographer: a freehand tool that draws glyph contours, grouped in Fontographer with Brush and Thickness on one toolbar button. Vexy Lines: draws the vector lines of a Handmade fill. Vexy Vextra: the Correcting Pencil, which redraws part of an existing path rather than only starting a new one. Name the app, and in Vextra use the full name Correcting Pencil.

Related: [Brush](#brush), [Knife](#knife), [Eraser](#eraser), [contour](#contour), [Fills](#fills).

<span id="power-nudge"></span>

### Power Nudge

Id: `power-nudge`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Power Nudge distributes a move through a larger part of a contour by
adjusting other nodes as well as the selected ones. FontLab and Vextra
provide this editing mode. It differs from Nudge, which adjusts nearby
handles. Power Nudge changes the geometry; inspect the result rather than
promising an exactly preserved outline or a finished curve.

Related: [node](#node), [handle](#handle), [Tunni line](#tunni-line), [Smart Corner](#smart-corner).

Translatable: yes.

The property bar is FontLab's context-sensitive strip of controls for the

<span id="remove-overlap"></span>

### Remove Overlap

Id: `remove-overlap`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Remove Overlap combines overlapping filled contour regions and removes their
internal boundaries. It changes contour structure, so keep an editable
source when the construction matters. Variable fonts can contain overlaps;
removing them independently from masters can break point correspondence and
interpolation. Apply the command or export option for the intended format
and workflow, rather than treating it as mandatory for every font.

Related: [contour](#contour), [winding direction](#winding-direction), [autotrace](#autotrace), [element](#element).

<span id="scissors"></span>

### Scissors

Id: `scissors`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

The Scissors tool works on the joins in a path, and what it does with them differs
by product. In FontLab it disconnects nodes into overlapping contours, creates
overlaps, adds simple ink traps, and builds looped corners, with a toolbox of
sub-tools under the Q shortcut. In Fontographer it breaks contours at selected
points or segments, the targeted counterpart to Knife. In Vexy Vextra it extends
and rebuilds path parts, building loop corners and joining open ends.

**Shared term:** FontLab: disconnects nodes, creates overlaps, adds ink traps, and builds looped corners. Fontographer: breaks a contour at a chosen point or segment, sharing the grouped cutting toolbar button with Knife. Vexy Vextra: extends and rebuilds path parts, joining open ends and building loop corners rather than only cutting. The name suggests cutting in every app and means something different in each, so name the app.

Related: [Knife](#knife), [node](#node), [contour](#contour), [Eraser](#eraser), [Pencil](#pencil).

### Sigma

Sigma is the Fontographer tool for variable font interpolation paths, sitting in

through its variation space rather than to edit a single outline. The name is a

the Sigma tool. It is a Fontographer term with no counterpart in the other

### Sketchboard

Id: `sketchboard`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab.

Translatable: no.

The Sketchboard is FontLab's unlimited canvas, a working surface where you can
place glyphs, drawings, and reference images side by side outside the fixed frame
of a glyph window. You use it to compare shapes, to sketch before committing, and

<span id="smart-corner"></span>

### Smart Corner

Id: `smart-corner`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: Smart Corners.

A Smart Corner is a live treatment applied at a node, so a corner can be rounded,
cut, or turned into an ink trap without the outline being redrawn. The treatment
stays editable: change the value and the corner updates, and the underlying
contour keeps its nodes. You apply it at the node rather than to the whole
contour. FontLab groups it with Ink Traps among its live filters, and Vextra
carries the same name.

### Transform

Id: `transform`. Status: approved. Applies to: fontlab, fontographer, vexy-lines, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

Transform moves, scales, rotates, slants, or flips a selection. In FontLab the
Transform panel applies a numeric transformation to glyphs, elements, or a
selection, and it can run across a whole font. In Fontographer the Transform tool
sits in the toolbar beside Meter and Sigma. In Vexy Lines the Transform tool
manipulates artwork on the canvas. A panel, a tool, and a tool again: name the app
and name the surface.

**Shared term:** FontLab: the Transform panel, a numeric transformation applied to a selection or across a font. Fontographer and Vexy Lines: a Transform tool on the canvas. ## Vector drawing

<span id="appearance-panel"></span>

### Appearance panel

Id: `appearance-panel`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-linestra, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

The Appearance panel is Illustrator's list of what is applied to the selected
object: its fills, its strokes, and its live effects, in the order they stack. You
send a reader there to confirm that a Vexy effect is present, to reorder it
against a stroke, or to remove it. It belongs to Illustrator rather than to our
products, so its name keeps Illustrator's capitalization and never takes a short
form of our making.

Related: [Live Effect](#live-effect), [Adobe Illustrator](#adobe-illustrator), [stroke](#stroke), [Fills](#fills).

<span id="autotrace"></span>

### autotrace

Id: `autotrace`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-lines, vexy-vextra, vexy-playlines.

Translatable: yes.

Autotrace converts a bitmap into vector paths by finding the edges in the image
and fitting curves to them. The result depends on what you feed it: a clean, high
contrast source traces well, and a soft or noisy one produces nodes nobody wants
to edit. You prepare the image first, trace, then simplify. Tracing to outlines
gives closed shapes with two edges, and tracing to centerlines gives single paths
with a stroke.

Related: [centerline](#centerline), [tonal map](#tonal-map), [contour](#contour), [Remove Overlap](#remove-overlap).

<span id="centerline"></span>

### centerline

Id: `centerline`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-lines, vexy-linestra, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

A centerline runs down the middle of a stroke rather than around its edge, so the
result is a single path with a stroke weight instead of a closed outline. Tracing
to centerlines suits line art, engravings, and anything that was drawn as strokes
in the first place. You choose it over outline tracing when the artwork will be
scaled or restroked later, since a centerline keeps one editable path where an
outline gives you two edges to maintain.

Related: [contour](#contour), [stroke](#stroke), [autotrace](#autotrace), [tonal map](#tonal-map).

<span id="fill-grid"></span>

### Fill Grid

Id: `fill-grid`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-linestra.

Translatable: yes.

The Fill Grid is the area of the Linestra panel where you choose a fill mode from
a grid of previews. Below it sit the parameter rows, which hold Thickness,
Spacing, Angle, Organic, Brightness, Contrast, and Source Levels. You pick in the
grid first, because the parameter rows change with the mode you choose. Both words
are capitalized: it is a named part of the interface rather than a description of
a grid.

Related: [fill mode](#fill-mode), [Vexy Linestra](#vexy-linestra), [panel](#panel), [Fills](#fills).

<span id="fill-mode"></span>

### fill mode

Id: `fill-mode`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-lines, vexy-linestra, vexy-playlines.

Translatable: yes.

A fill mode is one of the named techniques that turn a source image into line
work: Linear, Wave, Circular, Spiral, Halftone, Stipple, Hex, Contour, Low-Poly,
Dither, Wireframe, Fractal, String Art, and ASCII among them. Each reads the
brightness of the source and lays down geometry accordingly. You pick a mode, then
tune its parameters. Write the mode name with its own capitalization and the words
fill mode in lowercase after it.

Related: [Fills](#fills), [Fill Grid](#fill-grid), [tonal map](#tonal-map), [centerline](#centerline).

<span id="g2-continuity"></span>

### G2 continuity

Id: `g2-continuity`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

G2 continuity means two segments meet with matching curvature, not merely with
matching direction. G1 keeps the tangent continuous and can still show a break in
the reflection where the curvature jumps. G2 removes that break. Vextra marks G2
nodes and can enforce the condition as you edit. You reach for it on long, shallow
curves and on shapes that will be seen large, where the eye finds a curvature
break that it would miss at text size.

Related: [node](#node), [handle](#handle), [Tunni line](#tunni-line), [segment](#segment).

<span id="live-effect"></span>

### Live Effect

Id: `live-effect`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-linestra, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

A Live Effect is an Illustrator effect that stays editable on the object it is
applied to, listed in the Appearance panel and recalculated whenever the artwork
beneath it changes. Vexy Linestra runs as one, so its fills follow the source
artwork rather than freezing into it. You apply it from the Effect menu, under
Vexy, and you expand it only when you need the result as plain paths. Expanding
ends the link.

Related: [Adobe Illustrator](#adobe-illustrator), [Vexy Linestra](#vexy-linestra), [Appearance panel](#appearance-panel), [Fills](#fills).

<span id="stroke"></span>

### stroke

Id: `stroke`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-lines, vexy-linestra, vexy-vextra.

Translatable: yes.

A stroke is the line drawn along a path, with a weight, a colour, and an
alignment that puts it inside, outside, or centred on the path. It is not the
fill, which covers the area the path encloses. You set stroke weight and alignment
in Illustrator and the Vexy plug-ins generate stroked paths as their output. In
type design the same word means a main part of a letter, so name the context when
both senses could apply.

Related: [centerline](#centerline), [Fills](#fills), [contour](#contour), [Live Effect](#live-effect).

<span id="tonal-map"></span>

### tonal map

Id: `tonal-map`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-lines, vexy-linestra, vexy-playlines.

Translatable: yes.

Also written: tonal value.

A tonal map is the reading of brightness across a source image that a fill uses to
decide where to put geometry. Dark areas widen a strip or crowd a pattern; light
areas narrow or thin it. You control the mapping with the Brightness, Contrast,
and Source Levels rows, which change what counts as dark before any geometry is
drawn. Adjust the map rather than the fill when the result is uniformly too heavy
or too sparse.

Related: [fill mode](#fill-mode), [centerline](#centerline), [autotrace](#autotrace), [Fills](#fills).

<span id="tunni-line"></span>

### Tunni line

Id: `tunni-line`. Status: approved. Applies to: vexy-vextra.

Translatable: no.

Also written: Tunni control, pill.

A Tunni line joins the two handles of a curve segment, with a control on it that
Vextra draws as a pill. Drag the control and both handles move in proportion, so
the depth of the curve changes without its balance changing. You use it when a
curve is the right shape but the wrong fullness, which is the correction that is
slowest to make one handle at a time. Named after Eduardo Tunni, so keep the
capital T.

Related: [handle](#handle), [node](#node), [G2 continuity](#g2-continuity), [segment](#segment).
